iOS (iPhone and iPad)

An iOS app is an .ipa file. There are three ways to get the .ipa of a version you own. ipatool is the recommended one: it works on Windows, macOS and Linux and talks to Apple directly.

1. ipatool (recommended)

ipatool is free and open source. It signs in with your Apple ID and downloads the .ipa of an app your Apple ID has, straight from Apple. It does not change or unlock the app.

You need

  • Your Apple ID and password, and a device that receives its two-factor codes.
  • A Windows PC, Mac or Linux computer.
  • Fortnite on that Apple ID. ipatool can only fetch what the App Store in your Apple ID's country carries.

Install

  • macOS: brew install ipatool (with Homebrew).
  • Windows and Linux: download the archive for your system from ipatool's releases, unpack it, and open a terminal (PowerShell on Windows) in that folder. On Linux, chmod +x ipatool* first.

Steps

  1. Sign in: 
    ipatool auth login --email you@example.com
    It asks for your password, then the two-factor code sent to your devices. On Windows and Linux it may also ask for a passphrase to protect the saved sign-in on this computer; choose one and remember it.
  2. Find Fortnite and its bundle ID: 
    ipatool search "Fortnite" --limit 5
    Note the bundleID of Epic's Fortnite in the results.
  3. If the app is free and your Apple ID has never had it, add it to your purchases first: 
    ipatool purchase --bundle-identifier <bundle ID>
  4. Download the current version: 
    ipatool download --bundle-identifier <bundle ID> --output Fortnite.ipa
  5. Older versions. Newer ipatool versions can list the versions Apple still serves for an app you own, and download one by its id: 
    ipatool list-versions --bundle-identifier <bundle ID>
ipatool download --bundle-identifier <bundle ID> --external-version-id <id> --output Fortnite-old.ipa
    The list holds numbers, not version names. Download one and run retrora-archive identify on it to see which Fortnite version it is. Run ipatool --help to see what your version supports.

2. Old versions in an old iTunes library

Before iTunes 12.7 (September 2017), iTunes kept every app it synced as an .ipa. If an iPhone was ever synced with iTunes on a computer, its apps may still be there, including Fortnite copies from 2018 to 2020.

  1. On that old PC, open C:\Users\<you>\Music\iTunes\iTunes Media\Mobile Applications. On an old Mac, ~/Music/iTunes/iTunes Media/Mobile Applications.
  2. Look for Fortnite *.ipa. Each file is one version.
  3. Copy them to a safe drive. These are often the only copies of old iOS versions that exist.

3. Apple Configurator on a Mac

Apple Configurator is free in the Mac App Store. When it installs an app you own on a connected iPhone or iPad, it downloads the .ipa and keeps it in a cache for a moment.

  1. Install Apple Configurator, open it and sign in with your Apple ID (Account > Sign In).
  2. Connect the iPhone or iPad with a cable and trust the computer.
  3. Select the device, then Add > Apps, choose Fortnite and Add.
  4. When it says the app already exists on the device, leave that message open. The .ipa is now in ~/Library/Group Containers/K36BKF7T3D.group.com.apple.configurator/Library/Caches/Assets/TemporaryItems/MobileApps/. In Finder, Go > Go to Folder, paste that path and copy the .ipa out.
  5. Then choose Stop on the message.

4. Check and keep the copy

retrora-archive identify Fortnite.ipa
retrora-archive extract Fortnite.ipa Fortnite-ipa
retrora-archive manifest Fortnite-ipa Fortnite-ipa.json

identify shows the release and version. Keep the .ipa itself, plus the manifest. The game inside is encrypted for Apple IDs; the tool says so and leaves it closed.

5. Putting it back on a device

A copy from the App Store installs on devices signed in to the same Apple ID. On a Mac, Apple Configurator: Add > Apps > Choose from my Mac, then pick the .ipa. Fortnite's own servers only accept the newest version, so an old copy is an archive, not a way to play online.

6. Common problems

  • "license is required" or "not purchased": run ipatool purchase first (free apps only).
  • Search finds nothing: your Apple ID's country store does not carry Fortnite. ipatool uses that store.
  • Sign-in loops: run ipatool auth revoke, then sign in again and enter the newest two-factor code.
  • An iPhone backup has no apps in it: correct. Backups hold app data only; use one of the three ways above.
